# Splitty: first three dinner groups

Start with three people who already organize meals with friends. Ask each to try Splitty at one upcoming dinner, then expand toward ten hosts after fixing the problems the first groups encounter. Participation is voluntary. A meal is a completed trial when at least two people claim items and the group can agree on the final split.

## Invitation to copy and send yourself

> Hey — I’m building Splitty to make splitting a restaurant bill easier. Everyone opens a link, claims what they had, and gets their share of tax and tip. Would you try it at your next group meal and tell me where it gets confusing? Here’s a sample you can try without signing in: https://splitty.cc/demo
>
> You can create three manual bills per calendar month for free with Google sign-in. Your friends need no account or app. Receipt scanning is optional and requires Pro; you don’t need to buy it to help test the manual flow.

These are draft messages, not messages sent by Codex. Choose recipients you know and who actually have a group meal coming up. A declined invitation needs no follow-up.

## Before the meal

- Ask the host whether they are comfortable sharing brief feedback after the meal.
- Have the host try the sample, then create a small practice bill. Confirm everyone understands line totals versus quantity and shared dishes.
- Use one host and at least one guest on separate phones. Aim to include iPhone Safari and Android Chrome across the first three groups.
- If the group wants to test scanning, use an already-entitled host. Any complimentary Pro grant must be separately arranged, clearly time-limited, and never counted as a paid conversion.

## At the table

1. The host creates a bill, either manually or by scanning with Pro. Compare the item names, quantities, line totals, tax and tip with the receipt before sharing. Avoid counting an included service charge twice.
2. The host saves their creator link, joins under a recognizable name and shares the guest link. Keep private links inside the group.
3. At least two guests join and claim what they had. Include one shared dish or a quantity greater than one if the actual receipt has one.
4. Confirm nothing priced is left unclaimed and the displayed total matches the agreed receipt total.
5. Settle through the group's chosen payment method. Splitty's “mark as paid” is an acknowledgement; it does not transfer or verify money. Confirm money in the payment app before marking a share paid.
6. If a bill changes after anyone pays, review payments already sent before paying again. A cleared paid mark is not a request for a second full payment.

Observe where people hesitate without coaching immediately. If something prevents finishing, help the group complete its meal and record what happened. Do not ask people to send money just to test a button.

## Three feedback questions

- Could everyone finish the split? Where did someone need help?
- What took longer or felt less clear than your usual method?
- When you next organize a group meal, would you use Splitty again? Why?

Ask about repeat use after a relevant second meal, not just after an arbitrary number of days. A host who has not had another group meal has not yet had a chance to return.

## Report a problem

Send a brief description to hello@splitty.cc: the step, the phone/browser, what you expected and what happened. Start without a screenshot or private bill link; support can ask for the minimum additional detail needed. For general feedback, the three questions above are enough.

## Decisions after three meals

- If a group cannot finish, fix that specific problem before adding more groups.
- If everyone finishes but needs the same explanation, improve that screen or wording.
- If people finish unaided, invite the next few hosts toward the ten-host pilot.
- Keep the current plans stable while learning. Use the existing roadmap's targets as learning goals, not forecasts: eight of ten groups finish, four hosts return when they have another meal, and two guests become hosts.
